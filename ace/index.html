Why it exists

Almost a billion Africans live within reach of mobile internet and still do not use it

GSMA's Mobile Economy Africa 2026 report finds that almost 1 billion people in Africa, 63% of the population, live inside mobile broadband coverage but do not use mobile internet. AI that needs a smartphone, an app and a data plan does not reach them. A short code, a text message and a phone call still do.

Services such as Digital Green's FarmerChat, Jacaranda Health's PROMPTS and MTN Rwanda's voice AI show the demand. Each one rebuilds its own knowledge, channels and safety layer, and the knowledge stays locked inside. ACE is the shared, open layer underneath.

Read more in two essays: AI for Africa has to work on the phone people already own and Africa's farm data is open. The last mile to farmers is the work left to do.

What it solves

Six problems ACE solves today

Any phone

AI on a basic phone

A full USSD menu where every screen fits 182 characters, SMS replies within three segments, a voice menu and WhatsApp, all over the same checked knowledge. No app, no smartphone and no data plan needed.

Safety first

The right emergency number

Emergency language in English, Swahili or French gets the caller's own national numbers and a human handoff before anything else runs. ACE never gives a number from another country, and where no public source publishes one, it does not invent it.

Scam Shield

Mobile money scams

Forward a suspicious mobile money message and get an explained verdict. In Kenya it cites Safaricom's own guidance; elsewhere it gives no number it cannot cite. It never tells anyone a message is safe.

Cited answers

Advice people can trust

Crop pests and diseases, maize storage, danger signs in pregnancy and antenatal checks, drawn from WHO, FAO, CIMMYT, CABI, IITA, ICIPE and KALRO guidance. Every answer names its source, and draft or expired facts are withheld by default.

MCP server

Grounding for AI assistants

Any AI assistant can ask ACE for cited African context, scam checks and emergency numbers. When a language model writes the reply, ACE checks it stayed with the facts and falls back when it did not.

Privacy

Privacy by design

Phone numbers are stored only as salted hashes. One word, FORGET, FUTA or OUBLIER, deletes everything ACE holds about a person. Community tips wait for expert review and are never served until checked.

How it works

One engine behind every channel, and safety runs first

Thin adapters turn each channel into one message type. The engine keeps one pseudonymous profile per person across channels, runs the safety rail, then commands, Scam Shield, the V2 decision services and retrieval over sourced context packs, and composes an answer that fits the channel.

Grounded or silent

Nothing reaches a person unless it comes from a checked fact, a dated snapshot or is a fixed system message.

Honest about gaps

When no checked fact matches, or a country has no data for a service, ACE says so and names who to ask.

No lock in

Python standard library plus PyYAML. Any OpenAI compatible model server works, including models you host yourself.

Coverage

Thirteen countries, one engine, and every caller gets their own country's numbers

FAQ

Questions people ask about ACE

What is ACE?

ACE, the African Context Engine, is open source software that gives checked, local, citable knowledge for Africa on any phone. It serves USSD, SMS, voice, WhatsApp, the web and AI assistants through MCP, in English, Swahili and French, across 13 countries.

Can I dial ACE today?

Not as a public service yet. ACE is software that a ministry, NGO, telecom or startup deploys behind its own short code, phone line or WhatsApp number. Anyone can try every channel in the browser playground that ships with the code.

Does ACE need a smartphone or mobile data?

No. USSD, SMS and voice work on any mobile phone without an app or a data plan. WhatsApp and the web are there for people who have them.

Does ACE use a large language model?

It does not need one. By default ACE composes answers directly from checked facts, with no model and no API key. A deployment can add any OpenAI compatible model, and ACE then checks that the model stayed with the facts and falls back when it did not.

Which countries and languages does ACE cover?

Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Zimbabwe, Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Togo, in English, Swahili and French. V2 plans voice in Hausa and Yoruba as well.

What can the 2.0 preview answer?

Whether rain is likely each day this week in a person's area, whether an official warning is in force, the nearest public health facilities, what a food cost in the nearest market, when the next antenatal check is due, and the likely problem with maize, cassava, banana or tomato. Each answer cites a dated dataset or a sourced fact, and the services are labelled a preview until they pass expert review.

Is ACE free to use?

Yes. The code is under the Apache License 2.0 and the open context packs are under CC BY 4.0. Third party sources keep their own rights, and each is credited.

Is ACE a doctor, agronomist or bank?

No. ACE shares published guidance and tells people when and where to get human help. Its safety phrases and translations need review by clinicians and fluent speakers before a public launch, and each deployment is responsible for its own data protection obligations.

Who created ACE?

ACE was created by Tom Bruno, a Product and Ecosystem Leader in AI Ecosystems, as a personal project, and is maintained with the ACE contributors.
