ACE, the African Context Engine

Checked, local, citable knowledge for Africa, on any phone (USSD, SMS/RCS, voice, WhatsApp and MCP). ACE answers in eight languages across 34 African countries: English, Swahili, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Amharic, Afaan Oromo and Somali.

Every answer names the published source or dated dataset it came from. Emergencies get the caller's own national numbers before anything else runs. It works with no AI model at all.

Why it exists

Almost a billion Africans live within reach of mobile internet and still do not use it

Mobile broadband covers 92% of Africa's population, yet only 28% of people use mobile internet on their own devices, according to the GSMA's Digital Africa Index 2026. That leaves 64% of Africans within reach of the network but not using mobile internet, almost a billion people. AI that needs a smartphone, an app and a data plan does not reach them. A short code, a text message and a phone call still do.

Services such as Digital Green's FarmerChat, Jacaranda Health's PROMPTS and MTN Rwanda's Miss Baza show the demand. Each one rebuilds its own knowledge, channels and safety layer, and the knowledge stays locked inside. ACE is the shared, open layer underneath.

Read more in two essays: AI for Africa has to work on the phone people already own and Africa's farm data is open. The last mile to farmers is the work left to do.

What it solves

Six problems ACE solves today

Any phone

Answers on the phone people already own

USSD, SMS and voice come first, because they reach every phone with no app and no data: a USSD menu where every screen fits, SMS replies within three segments and a keypad voice line. People with a smartphone and data can choose WhatsApp, or RCS where their carrier supports it, to see the sources under every answer, with suggested replies on RCS. RCS falls back to SMS, so a reply never depends on RCS.

Safety first

The right emergency number

Emergency language in any of the eight languages gets the caller's own national numbers and a human handoff before anything else runs. ACE never gives a number from another country, and where no public source publishes one, it does not invent it.

Scam Shield

Mobile money scams

Forward a suspicious mobile money message and get an explained verdict. In Kenya it cites Safaricom's own guidance; elsewhere it gives no number it cannot cite. It never tells anyone a message is safe.

Cited answers

Advice people can trust

Crop pests and diseases, maize storage, danger signs in pregnancy and antenatal checks, drawn from WHO, FAO, CIMMYT, CABI, IITA, ICIPE and KALRO guidance. Every answer names its source, and draft or expired facts are withheld by default.

MCP server

Grounding for AI assistants

Any AI assistant can ask ACE for cited African context, scam checks and emergency numbers. When a language model writes the reply, ACE checks it stayed with the facts and falls back when it did not.

Privacy

Privacy by design

Phone numbers are stored only as salted hashes. One word, such as FORGET, FUTA or OUBLIER, deletes everything ACE holds about a person. Community tips wait for expert review and are never served until checked.

How it works

One engine behind every channel, and safety runs first

Thin adapters turn each channel into one message type. The engine keeps one pseudonymous profile per person across channels, runs the safety rail, then commands, Scam Shield, the V2 decision services and retrieval over sourced context packs, and composes an answer that fits the channel.

Grounded or silent

Nothing reaches a person unless it comes from a checked fact, a dated snapshot or is a fixed system message.

Honest about gaps

When no checked fact matches, or a country has no data for a service, ACE says so and names who to ask.

No lock in

Python standard library plus PyYAML. Any OpenAI compatible model server works, including models you host yourself.

Release 2.2 preview · Speech and translation

ACE is now tested on real African speech, and its voice line can now speak Amharic and Somali

Release 2.2 runs ACE's language guess, safety rail and service router on 12,333 sentences of real speech text from WAXAL, the open African speech corpus from Google Research and seven African partners. ACE recognises Amharic, Swahili and Afaan Oromo in 93% to 100% of those sentences, and the safety rail fired on 3 ordinary sentences out of 12,333. The voice line reads English, Swahili, French, Portuguese and Arabic with Google Cloud voices, and plays Amharic and Somali audio made with Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS. No Google model speaks Afaan Oromo yet, so Oromo callers get the full reply on SMS and USSD in their own language.

Optional Google Cloud providers hear spoken questions with Chirp 3, speak replies and give fluent reviewers a back translation of every string. Machine translation never answers a person. The gap is named: ACE still reads many languages it does not speak as Swahili, now in 45.8% of sentences, down from 54.0%. Read the speech and translation guide and the WAXAL results.

FAQ

Questions people ask about ACE

What is ACE?

ACE, the African Context Engine, is open source software that gives checked, local, citable knowledge for Africa on any phone (USSD, SMS/RCS, voice, WhatsApp and MCP), in eight languages across 34 countries. AI assistants reach it through MCP, and other services through its API.

Can I dial ACE today?

Not as a public service yet. ACE is software that a ministry, NGO, telecom or startup deploys behind its own short code, phone line, WhatsApp number or RCS agent. Anyone can try the USSD and SMS menus in the browser playground that ships with the code.

Does ACE need a smartphone or mobile data?

No. USSD, SMS and voice are the default, because they reach any mobile phone with no app and no data plan. People with a smartphone and data can choose WhatsApp, or RCS where their carrier supports it, to see the sources under every answer, with suggested replies on RCS. RCS falls back to SMS, so a reply never depends on RCS. Apps and web pages can also be built on the ACE API.

Does ACE work by voice in African languages?

Yes, in seven of ACE's eight languages. The keypad voice line works on any phone. It reads English, Swahili, French, Portuguese and Arabic with Google Cloud voices, and plays Amharic and Somali audio made with Gemini TTS when the operator adds a Gemini API key. Afaan Oromo has no voice yet, so Oromo callers hear the next language of their country with a voice and get the full reply on SMS and USSD in Afaan Oromo. Spoken questions work through Google speech recognition where a model exists.

Does ACE use a large language model?

It does not need one. By default ACE composes answers directly from checked facts, with no model and no API key. A deployment can add any OpenAI compatible model, and ACE then checks that the model stayed with the facts and falls back when it did not.

Which countries and languages does ACE cover?

34 countries: Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Zimbabwe, Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Togo, DR Congo, Malawi, Burundi, Cameroon, Benin, Zambia, Chad, Guinea, Burkina Faso, South Sudan, Ethiopia, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau, Angola, Somalia, Sudan, Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Morocco and Tunisia. Eight languages: English, Swahili, French, Portuguese, Arabic, Amharic, Afaan Oromo and Somali. V2 plans voice in Hausa and Yoruba as well.

What can the decision services answer?

Whether rain is likely each day this week in a person's area, whether an official warning is in force, the nearest public health facilities, what a food cost in the nearest market, when the next antenatal check is due, and the likely problem with maize, cassava, banana or tomato. Each answer cites a dated dataset or a sourced fact, and the services are labelled a preview until they pass expert review.

Is ACE free to use?

Yes. The code is under the Apache License 2.0 and the open context packs are under CC BY 4.0. Third party sources keep their own rights, and each is credited.

Is ACE a doctor, agronomist or bank?

No. ACE shares published guidance and tells people when and where to get human help. Its safety phrases and translations need review by clinicians and fluent speakers before a public launch, and each deployment is responsible for its own data protection obligations.

Who created ACE?

ACE was created by Tom Bruno, a Product and Ecosystem Leader in AI Ecosystems at Google, as a personal project, and is maintained with the ACE contributors.
